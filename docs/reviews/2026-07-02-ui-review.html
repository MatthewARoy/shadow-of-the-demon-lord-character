UI Review · July 2, 2026

Ledger of the Damned

Full walk-through of the live app — all seven tabs, desktop (1280px) and mobile (375px), driven with a real imported character.

The visual identity is the app's biggest asset and nothing below proposes changing it. These are flow, clarity, and reach improvements, each with a mock rendered in the app's own palette. Mocks are HTML recreations, not screenshots — treat them as sketches of intent, not pixel specs.

What's already working

A real identity

The parchment sheet with the pentagram stat wheel is genuinely distinctive — nobody will mistake this for a generic character tool. The gothic voice ("Ask, and the law shall answer") is consistent everywhere.

Decisions surface themselves

"Unresolved Fates" puts exactly the actionable choices in front of the player — the imported character's two unpicked professions were impossible to miss.

Provenance everywhere

Every derived number can explain itself; the exchange chips even preserve an 8-step swap chain. That's rare and worth building on (see finding 5).

Quietly considerate details

Casting pips, learnable-now filtering, print styles for the paper sheet, offline-capable rules search, encumbrance limits from Strength.

1Mobile header spends the whole first screen on chrome

Impact: highEffort: Mindex.html · css/app.css

At 375px the masthead, roster select, +New, Samples, Export, Import, and a lone floating delete ✕ stack to roughly 500px — a full viewport of controls before any character content. The delete button ends up visually orphaned on its own row, right where a thumb expects a primary action.

Collapse to two rows. Keep the title as a compact monogram row, keep the roster select full-width beside a ⋯ menu that holds New / Samples / Export / Import / Delete. Destructive delete moves inside the menu — off the first screen, harder to fat-finger.

2Mobile tab bar hides Dice & Lookup with no affordance

Impact: highEffort: Scss/app.css

The seven tabs overflow at 375px and the strip clips clean at the edge — Dice and Lookup simply don't exist unless you happen to try swiping a row that doesn't look swipeable.

Make the cut visible. Let the strip scroll (overflow-x: auto + scrollbar-width: none), add a right-edge gradient fade, and nudge the layout so the last visible tab is always half-cropped — a cropped label is the affordance. scroll-snap-type: x proximity keeps taps landing cleanly.

3Keyboard & screen-reader reach: five gaps, all cheap

Impact: highEffort: S–M

The app is mouse-first in a few places that matter at a real table (tablets, external keyboards, screen readers). None of these change the visual design:

  • Roll results are silent. Toasts render without aria-live, so a screen reader never hears a roll outcome. One attribute on the toast container. js/ui/toast.js
  • The pentagram's rollable nodes are <div>s. Strength/Agility/Perception rolls are unreachable by keyboard. Render rollables as <button> with the existing focus outline. js/ui/sheet.js
  • The tab bar is plain buttons. Add role="tablist" / aria-selected and arrow-key movement. index.html · js/main.js
  • Long spell/path text expands only by clicking a <p>. Wrap the clamp toggle in a button or give it tabindex + Enter handling. js/ui/spells.js · paths.js · lookup.js
  • The spell modal doesn't trap or move focus. It already has role="dialog" and Esc — send focus in on open, return it on close. js/ui/spells.js

4Play-tracking controls are small, scattered text chips

Impact: mediumEffort: Sjs/ui/sheet.js

Damage, Insanity, and Corruption — the three numbers that change constantly during play — are adjusted through six small − / + text chips in two rows under the pentagram. Mid-session (the "someone just hit me" moment) they're easy to mis-tap, and the value each chip affects sits in a circle elsewhere on the wheel.

Group each tracked value with its own stepper. Big round ± targets, the value between them, Heal/Rest staying adjacent. Same parchment/oxblood language; nothing else on the sheet moves.

5Provenance lives in a far-away panel instead of on the number

Impact: mediumEffort: Mjs/ui/sheet.js

The engine knows exactly why Defense is 18 — and prints it in a flat "Provenance" panel below the fold, disconnected from the circles it explains. Now that equipped armor and shields flow into Defense, "why is this number what it is" will be asked more, not less.

Put the answer on the number. Hover (desktop) or tap-and-hold (touch) on any stat circle shows its breakdown; the panel below can stay for print. This also gives the new armor math a place to explain itself.

6Exchange history reads as noise at the bottom of the grimoire

Impact: mediumEffort: Sjs/ui/spells.js

The imported character's eight spell exchanges render as eight separate ⇄ A → B ✕ chips wrapping across two rows. The data is excellent — the presentation makes a feature look like leftover debug output, and each ✕ (an undo) sits one errant click away.

Collapse it into a ledger entry. One disclosure line summarizing the count; expanding shows each exchange as a sentence with its undo. Chains that fold into one another (Rune of Invisibility → … → Swiftness) can render as a single lineage line — the provenance the engine already tracks.

7Archive filters give no feedback on what's filtered

Impact: mediumEffort: Sjs/ui/spells.js

The Archive's filter bar is five dropdowns and three toggles. Once two or three are set, nothing tells you how many of the 1,120 spells survived, which filters are active, or how to get back to everything — the dropdowns just quietly hold state.

Add one summary line under the bar: result count, each active filter as a removable chip, and a clear-all. This is the cheapest possible orientation aid and it works for the Paths browser too.

8Armory sprawls; the equip checkbox is unlabeled

Impact: mediumEffort: Mjs/ui/gear.js

Two related issues. The armory is one very long single column — reaching Heavy Melee means scrolling past every basic weapon, while the possessions column sits mostly empty beside it. And in possessions, the first column is a bare checkbox with no header: nothing says it means equipped, or what equipping does — which matters now that armor actually drives Defense and Speed.

Name the column and show the consequence. An "Equipped" toggle with an effect line ("Defense 13 · replaces Agility") turns a mystery checkbox into a decision. For the armory: sticky category tabs and a denser two-column row layout; the encumbrance line becomes a small meter.

9Dice tab underuses the room it has

Impact: lowEffort: Sjs/ui/dice.js

Two half-empty panels float over a screen of dark background; the roll ledger ("The dice are silent") sits far from the buttons that feed it. The attribute quick-roll chips with live modifiers are the best part and deserve the visual lead.

One shared panel, roller left, running ledger right — most recent roll writ large with its boon/bane math, history beneath it. Add Enter-to-roll on the damage-expression input if it's not already there.

10Sheet description box: fixed height, OS scrollbar on parchment

Impact: lowEffort: Sjs/ui/sheet.js · css/app.css

The imported character's ten-line backstory gets four visible rows and a default OS scrollbar — the one modern-UI artifact on an otherwise immaculate parchment.

Let it grow. field-sizing: content with a max-height where supported (or the classic scrollHeight resize on input), plus a thin parchment-toned scrollbar for the capped case.

11Sample picks silently multiply roster entries

Impact: lowEffort: Sjs/main.js

Choosing from "Samples…" imports a fresh copy into the roster every time — browse three samples and your roster has three new souls, with no confirmation and no visible way back except delete-each.

Announce and offer the exit. The toast system already exists: "Torga Stonejaw joined the roster — Undo". Numbering repeat imports ("Torga Stonejaw (2)") keeps the select legible.

12Lookup highlighting — fixed during this review

Fixed on this branchjs/ui/lookup.js

Searching "boons and banes" highlighted every and — including inside hands and standard — and let function words inflate section scores. Stopwords are now dropped from scoring and highlighting (with a fallback when the whole query is stopwords), and highlights anchor to word starts. Verified live: only boons and banes mark, ranking unchanged.
